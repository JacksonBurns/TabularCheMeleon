## 1. What we tested

The prior report established RBF-kernel regression (KRR/GP) on frozen CheMeleon
2048-dim embeddings, with an SVD-whitening (Mahalanobis) metric ablation. The
whitened KRR beat the TabICL v2 reference FM on 4/9 polaris benchmarks and
established that the remaining gap to CheMeleon end-to-end (best on 6/9) is a
frozen-embedding information ceiling, not an architecture limit.

This round explores three axes the user asked for:

- **(c) Heteroscedastic noise** - input-dependent `sigma(x)` fit by iteratively
  reweighted least squares (IRWLS) with a linear-in-features log-noise model
  (Rasmussen & Williams GPML Ch. 5.3; Binois 2016).
- **(d) Alternative kernels** - Matérn-5/2, Spectral Mixture Kernel (SMK, fit by
  GP marginal MLE; Wilson & Adams 2013), Tanimoto on the count-FP block
  (Szedmak 2020, Mahé 2007), and Tanimoto(FP) × RBF(desc) product kernel
  (Szabó 2017, Kim 2016 MultiDK).
- **Descriptor embedding** - a 2373-dim classical feature vector: 2048-dim
  radius-3 Morgan **count** fingerprint (not binary) + 325 RDKit 2D descriptors,
  as an alternative to the 2048-dim frozen CheMeleon GNN embedding.

All models remain **inductive** (test points never see each other),
**closed-form**, and **cannot collapse** to the mean predictor.

## 5. Per-kernel findings

### 5.1 Spectral Mixture Kernel is the standout

SMK (M=3, fit by MLE) is the single most effective kernel extension:

- Wins or co-wins on **solu, hppb, perm, rclint, hclint** (5/9).
- On **perm** (n=1919), the descriptor-space SMK (0.774) beats the best
  CheMeleon-space config (0.759) - the only benchmark where the classical
  descriptor embedding + adaptive kernel outperforms the GNN embedding.
- The SMK's ability to fit multiple lengthscale modes (one per "regime" in
  chemical space) is the mechanism: it adapts to each benchmark's actual
  correlation structure rather than assuming a single Gaussian smoothness.
- **No prior paper applies SMK to molecular fingerprints or CheMeleon-style
  GNN embeddings** (confirmed by lit search; closest is CatBOX 2025, which
  uses SMK for catalyst design BO, not molecular property prediction).

### 5.2 Matérn-5/2 helps on data-scarce tasks

- **rppb** (n=111): Matérn/white = **0.796**, the single best score in the
  entire table (beats KRR/white 0.788, TabICL 0.779, CheMeleon 0.662).
- The Matérn's finite differentiability (ν=5/2) is a softer inductive bias
  than RBF's infinite differentiability, which helps when there are too few
  points to estimate the RBF's sharp local structure.
- **Manzhos 2023** (arXiv:2311.10790) warns that Matérn KRR degenerates to
  low-order polynomial regression in high dimension. We see this: on
  data-rich tasks (rclint n=2218, hclint n=2229), Matérn underperforms SMK
  and RBF. The degeneration is real but doesn't hurt on small n.

### 5.3 Heteroscedastic noise: mixed bag

- **Improves** on rppb (white: 0.788→0.795), hppb (raw: 0.653→0.699),
  pkis2-ret (raw: 810→807).
- **Worsens** on solu (raw: 0.631→0.628), perm (raw: 0.759→0.750),
  rclint (raw: 0.712→0.701), hclint (raw: 0.677→0.675).
- Net: **not a clear win**. The IRWLS linear log-noise model is too simple
  for 2048-dim inputs (2049 params for the noise function, fitted on ≤1200
  points). On small benchmarks it overfits the noise surface; on large ones
  it adds negligible signal.
- **No molecular-specific heteroscedastic GP paper found** in the lit search
  (closest: Ozbayram 2024 for materials structure-property). This is a gap,
  but the naive implementation doesn't close it productively.

### 5.4 Tanimoto(FP) and product kernel: weak on ADME

- **Tanimoto on the count-FP block alone** is poor: rppb = **-0.124**
  (negative correlation!), hppb = 0.363. The Jaccard similarity on raw
  fingerprint counts doesn't capture the property-relevant structure as well
  as the Euclidean-in-whitened-space metric.
- **Tanimoto × RBF(desc) product** is slightly better (rppb = -0.075, hppb
  = 0.334) but still far below the CheMeleon-space kernels.
- **Why:** The Tanimoto kernel measures *substructure overlap* (which
  molecular fragments are shared). ADME properties (solubility, plasma
  protein binding, CYP inhibition) are *emergent* properties of the whole
  molecule, not additive over fragments. The CheMeleon GNN embedding, by
  contrast, has already integrated the substructure into a holistic
  representation. The Tanimoto kernel is the right tool for *activity
  cliffs* and *scaffold hopping* (where shared substructure is the signal),
  not for continuous ADME properties.
- **Szedmak 2020** (arXiv:2007.05943) generalizes Tanimoto to count
  fingerprints, which is what we use. The kernel is mathematically sound;
  the feature is just the wrong one for these tasks.

### 5.5 Descriptor embedding vs CheMeleon embedding

| | Descriptor (2373-d) | CheMeleon (2048-d) |
|---|--:|--:|
| best config | desc/smk (perm 0.774) | white/matern52 (rppb 0.796) |
| avg rank (of 14, 9 polaris) | 4.78 | 1.22 |
| beats TabICL v2 | 2/9 (perm, rclint) | 6/9 |
| beats CheMeleon e2e | 0/9 | 1/9 (rppb) |

- The **descriptor embedding is consistently worse** than the CheMeleon
  embedding across all kernels. The GNN's learned representation captures
  property-relevant structure that hand-crafted fingerprints + descriptors
  miss.
- The one place the descriptor is competitive is **perm** (permeability,
  n=1919), where desc/smk (0.774) beats the *best CheMeleon-space GP config*
  (0.759) - but still not CheMeleon e2e (0.822). Permeability is strongly
  driven by lipophilicity (LogP) and hydrogen-bonding capacity - exactly the
  properties that RDKit descriptors encode explicitly. The GNN embedding may
  dilute this signal in its 2048-dim holistic representation.
- **This is the expected result** and validates the CheMeleon pipeline: the
  learned embedding is a better feature space for GP/kernel inference than
  classical descriptors, with the caveat that for properties with strong
  explicit descriptor correlates (LogP, HBA/HBD), the classical features
  remain competitive.

## 7. Literature positioning

Full citation list: `/home/jackson/gp_kernel_litsearch/REPORT.md` (49 refs).

**Closest prior art:**
- **FlowMO** (Moss & Griffiths 2020, arXiv:2010.01118): GP on RDKit
  fingerprints/descriptors. The canonical tool. We extend it with adaptive
  kernels (SMK, Matérn) and a head-to-head vs GNN embeddings.
- **Parkinson 2023** (arXiv:2302.03294): linear-scaling GP kernels beat
  deep learning on small-molecule regression with UQ. Validates our
  "kernels are competitive" thesis.
- **Jamali 2025** (arXiv:2510.14217): spectral analysis of molecular
  embeddings; kernels remain competitive in low-data regimes.
- **Szedmak 2020** (arXiv:2007.05943): Tanimoto generalized to count
  fingerprints - the mathematical basis for our Tanimoto(FP) kernel.
- **Wilson & Adams 2013** (arXiv:1302.4245): the SMK paper. No prior
  application to molecular property prediction found.

**Gaps we occupy:**
1. **SMK on molecular fingerprints / GNN embeddings** - no prior paper.
2. **Heteroscedastic GP for molecular property prediction** - no prior
   paper (closest: Ozbayram 2024 for materials).
3. **Fingerprint × descriptor product kernel + GP for ADME** - no prior
   paper (closest: Kim 2016 MultiDK, which ensembles kernels rather than
   taking their product).
4. **Training-free kernel on a frozen foundation-model embedding** - the
   "frozen CheMeleon + adaptive kernel" framing is unoccupied.

## 8. Summary

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **SMK is the best adaptive kernel** | Wins/co-wins 5/9 polaris; beats RBF on all data-rich tasks |
| **Matérn-5/2 helps on small data** | rppb 0.796 (best score overall); degrades on large n (Manzhos 2023) |
| **Heteroscedastic noise: no clear win** | Mixed; IRWLS linear model too simple for 2048-d inputs |
| **Tanimoto(FP) wrong tool for ADME** | Substructure overlap ≠ emergent property; rppb -0.124 |
| **CheMeleon embedding > descriptors** | 6/9 vs 2/9 beating TabICL; exception: perm (LogP-driven) |
| **Frozen-embedding ceiling holds** | CheMeleon e2e best on 8/9; no kernel breaks it |
| **Beats TabICL v2 on 6/9** (up from 4/9) | SMK + Matérn extend the win |

**Highest-value next step:** The SMK's per-benchmark adaptivity is the
mechanism that pushes from 4/9 to 6/9 TabICL wins. The natural extension is
to **feed the SVD-whitened CheMeleon space into TabICL v2 / TabPFN** (the
original proposal from the prior session) - now with the knowledge that the
whitened space + adaptive kernel is the right geometry, and the FM can learn
the in-context skill on top of it. The SMK results suggest the FM's
attention would also benefit from operating in a decorrelated space.
